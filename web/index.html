Two ways a conversation opens

You both said yes

You show interest in someone. If they show interest back, the chat opens. Free, and always the first route we offer.

You did not want to wait

Pay once and write to a single person without the match. That fee buys the introduction and nothing else.

Profiles are public and free to read. Contact stays closed until one of the two routes opens it, so nobody gets written to merely for being visible.

Nobody pays for the day itself. Not a fee, not a tip, not a gift that everyone knows is a fee.

It is what separates a cultural exchange from guided tourism, and in Italy that line is the law (L. 190/2023). A host who asks to be paid for their time is removed.

Two roles, and you can hold both

Hosting
You live in an Italian city and you have an afternoon. You decide who, when, and whether at all. Some hosts also offer a bed, which carries real obligations towards the local Questura, spelled out before that option can be switched on.
Visiting
You are passing through, from anywhere. You read what someone actually proposes to do with you, not a menu of tours, and you ask.

Nobody owes anybody a return visit. Being hosted puts you in no debt, and never hosting locks you out of nothing.

What stands behind a stranger

  • An identity, checked. Every member verifies a document before they can write to anyone. It also proves they are over eighteen.
  • A block that holds. Blocking closes the conversation in both directions, takes it out of both inboxes, and hides each profile from the other.
  • Reviews from people who met. Only the two sides of an exchange that actually happened can review each other.
  • A ban that follows the person. A removed member cannot return with a second account on the same document.

Opening in Milan first

Hosts register in Italy, the one country whose rules we have checked. Travellers can come from anywhere.
